You are reading the raw OCR text of a published archaeology journal article
reporting on a lithic (stone tool) assemblage from one or more excavated
sites. Your job is to find this article's SECTION HEADINGS.

ABOUT THE INPUT:
The text is given page by page, each page introduced by a "=== Page N ==="
marker. The layout software failed to recognize this document's structure, so
nothing is marked up: headings appear as ordinary lines of text, exactly as
printed in the article.

The text also preserves the original line breaks of the printed page, and the
article is typeset in columns. A single sentence is therefore split across
many short lines, and a short line is NOT evidence of a heading. For example:

    The results point to its relevance for the cha-
    Middle Dorset, and to its potential contribu-

Those are mid-sentence fragments of running prose, not headings. Real headings
are short, grammatically complete labels for a section of the article, and the
text that follows one reads as the start of a new topic.

WHAT COUNTS AS A HEADING:
- Section names of the kind journal articles use — "Introduction", "The Site",
  "Materials and Methods", "Raw Material Procurement", "Lithic Analysis",
  "Results", "Discussion", "Conclusion", "Acknowledgements" — whether or not
  they are numbered ("3.2 Core Reduction").
- Site- or assemblage-specific section names, which are often the most
  important ones here: "The GhGk-63 Site", "Layer C Assemblage", "Excavations
  at Locus 2".

WHAT DOES NOT COUNT:
- Mid-sentence line fragments, including ones that begin with a capital letter
  or end with a hyphen.
- The article title, author names, affiliations, journal name, running heads,
  page numbers, DOIs, URLs, copyright and access statements.
- Figure and table captions ("Fig. 3. Artifacts from...", "Table 2. Attribute
  data...").
- Bibliography entries and any heading inside the references section.
- A line that is simply a proper noun mentioned in passing within a paragraph.

RULES:
- Return each heading EXACTLY as it appears in the text, character for
  character. Do not tidy, expand, renumber, translate or re-case it. The text
  you return is checked against the page, and a heading that does not match
  the page verbatim is discarded.
- Give the page number from the "=== Page N ===" marker the heading appears
  under.
- If the article genuinely has no headings on a page, return none for it.
- Prefer omitting a doubtful line to including it. A missed heading costs less
  here than a fragment of prose promoted to a section boundary.

Return ONLY a JSON object — no explanation, no markdown:
{
  "headings": [
    {"page": 3, "text": "<heading exactly as printed>"}
  ]
}
