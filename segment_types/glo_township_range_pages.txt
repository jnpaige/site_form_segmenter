You are analyzing a window of pages from a 19th-century GLO (General Land
Office) survey field-note volume, OCR'd from handwritten manuscript. The
volume records section-line surveys for multiple Townships and Ranges, one
after another, each block running for anywhere from a few pages to over a
hundred. Your job is to determine, for every page shown below, which
Township/Range/Section it belongs to.

ABOUT THE FORMATS YOU WILL SEE:
Surveyors marked each page with a running header identifying the current
Township and Range, but the exact wording varies within the SAME volume —
do not assume only one format is used:
  - A compact repeating stamp, e.g. "T. 2N R. 6W S.W.D.", "T3N R4W N.W.D.",
    "T. 8 N. R. 7 W.". This usually appears as a short, isolated line, often
    near the top of the page.
  - A verbose header used to introduce a new block, e.g. "Field Notes of
    Township No. 3 North in Range No. 4 West of the Basis Meridian", or
    "North Boundary of Township No 13 in Range No. 6 West".
Both forms are equally valid evidence of which Township/Range a page belongs
to — treat them the same way. OCR frequently garbles individual characters
(digits misread as letters or vice versa, e.g. "2" as "Z", "1" as "I" or
"l"), garbles the direction letters (N/S, E/W, and E/W sometimes misread as
each other or as F/V), and garbles township/range/section numbers generally.
Use surrounding context (nearby numbers, the overall flow of the document,
what block came immediately before) to resolve ambiguous OCR rather than
requiring a perfectly clean match.

A page may also carry a district code such as "S.W.D." (Southwest District),
"N.W.D." (Northwest District), "S.E.D.", "N.E.D." — record it if visible,
leave it blank if not.

IMPORTANT — DO NOT CONFUSE A LISTING WITH A RUNNING HEADER:
Some pages (often near the start of a volume) are a table of contents or
survey index, listing MANY different Townships and Ranges assigned to
different surveyors, one after another in a short list. A page like this is
NOT itself part of any of those Townships' field notes — label it based on
what its own surrounding content actually is (often it won't belong to any
Township at all; that's fine, see NO_MATCH below). Only treat a
Township/Range mention as the page's own assignment if the page's actual
substantive content (the survey line descriptions, bearing trees, section
boundaries) is describing that Township — not just because the words appear
somewhere on the page.

WATCH FOR CROSS-REFERENCES TO A DIFFERENT TOWNSHIP — THIS IS THE SINGLE
BIGGEST SOURCE OF ERROR, READ THIS CAREFULLY:
A common convention in these volumes is a page that says something like "For
the North Boundary of this Township see the Field Notes of the East
Boundary of Township No. 1 North in Range No. 4 East." This sentence
mentions Township 1 North, Range 4 East — but the page containing this
sentence almost always belongs to a DIFFERENT township (e.g. Township 1
North, Range 2 East), and is simply directing the reader to consult that
OTHER township's notes for where their shared boundary runs. Do not let the
township/range number inside a "see the field notes of..." / "see the
Field Notes of the [direction] Boundary of..." sentence override the page's
real assignment.

The single most reliable signal for a page's real assignment is its running
header stamp (e.g. "T. 1N R. 2E", "TIN RZE", "R 2E") — the short line
repeated at the top of nearly every page — NOT any township/range number
that appears inside a full sentence of body text citing another township.
If a page has its own header stamp, trust the stamp over any number
mentioned in a cross-reference sentence elsewhere on that same page, even if
the cross-reference is only one number "off" from the stamp (e.g. stamp says
Range 2 East, cross-reference sentence mentions Range 4 East — trust the
stamp: this page is Range 2 East, and Range 4 East is simply the neighboring
township being cited, not this page's own assignment).

CONTINUATION PAGES:
Most pages do NOT repeat the header at all — they simply continue the survey
line descriptions from the previous page with no header text of their own.
For these pages, assign the SAME Township/Range as the nearest preceding
page that does show clear evidence, UNLESS the content itself signals a
change (e.g. it starts describing a different Range's boundary, or a new
"Field Notes of Township..." header appears partway through this page).
Use your judgment about narrative continuity, not just the presence or
absence of a header line.

SECTIONS:
Separately from Township/Range, record the Section number for each page if
the text mentions one — most commonly as "___ Boundary of Section No. X"
(where ___ is North/South/East/West), but also accept other clear phrasings
that unambiguously name a section number in the context of describing that
section's boundary or survey line. If a page's text spans a transition
between two sections (the previous section's description ends and a new
one begins on the same page), report the section that most of the page's
substantive content belongs to. If no section is identifiable, leave it
null — do not guess.

NO_MATCH:
If a page is a cover sheet, blank, pure OCR noise, or otherwise has no
identifiable Township/Range content of its own AND no clear preceding
context to continue from, set township to null.

WATCH FOR A DIGIT+DIRECTION MERGE, ESPECIALLY "S" FOR SOUTH:
Handwritten "1 S." (Township 1 South) or "2 S." (Township 2 South) is
frequently OCR'd with the South direction letter crammed against the digit,
producing what LOOKS like a clean two-digit number ending in 5 — e.g. "1S"
misread as "15", "2S" misread as "25", "3S" misread as "35". If you see an
apparent township number in the teens or twenties (e.g. "15", "25") ending
in 5, be suspicious: check the surrounding text for an explicit "South"
elsewhere (e.g. "Township No. 1 South", "South of the 31st of Latitude").
If you find one, the real township number is very likely just the leading
digit (so "15" -> Township 1, direction South, NOT Township 15), not the
full two-digit reading. This corpus's townships are consistently small
numbers (typically well under 10) — a sudden jump to a teens/twenties
township number is itself a signal to re-examine whether this merge
happened, rather than taking the apparent number at face value. The same
caution applies to any apparent number ending in 0 near a possible "North"
misread, though this is less common than the "S" case.

STRICT OUTPUT FORMAT — READ CAREFULLY:
township and range must ALWAYS be either a clean number as a string ("1",
"23") or null. NEVER put a letter, a garbled OCR fragment, a partial word,
or anything else that is not a clean number into the township or range
field. township_dir must ALWAYS be exactly "N", exactly "S", or null —
NEVER any other letter or fragment. range_dir must ALWAYS be exactly "E",
exactly "W", or null. If you cannot confidently determine a clean number or
a clean direction letter for a page, use null for that field (and for
township/range/township_dir/range_dir together if you are not confident of
the township or range at all) rather than guessing or copying a garbled
fragment from the page text into the field. A wrong or malformed value is
worse than null — null is always an acceptable, correct answer when you are
unsure.
