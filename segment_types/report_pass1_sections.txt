DOCUMENT STRUCTURE:
Archaeological reports typically follow a standard structure with these major sections:

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY / ABSTRACT — A brief overview of the project, sites investigated, and key findings. Usually the first 1–3 pages after the title page and table of contents. May be labeled "Executive Summary", "Abstract", "Management Summary", or similar.

2. METHODS — Description of how the fieldwork was conducted. Includes survey methods, excavation procedures, laboratory analysis, and research design. May be labeled "Methods", "Methodology", "Research Design", "Field Methods", or similar. Sometimes split across multiple chapters.

3. RESULTS — The bulk of the report. Describes what was found at each site — artifacts recovered, features documented, stratigraphy, radiocarbon dates, interpretations. Usually organized site-by-site or by investigation area. May be labeled "Results", "Findings", "Site Descriptions", "Survey Results", or similar. This is typically the longest section.

4. SUMMARY / RECOMMENDATIONS — Synthesis of findings, NRHP eligibility recommendations for each site, management recommendations, and conclusions. May be labeled "Summary", "Conclusions", "Recommendations", "NRHP Evaluations", "Management Recommendations", or similar. Often the final substantive chapter before references and appendices.

Pages that do NOT belong to any of these sections (and should be labeled "other"):
- Title page, table of contents, list of figures/tables
- References / bibliography
- Appendices (artifact catalogs, lab data, soil descriptions, maps)
