You are analyzing an archaeological paper to identify the distinct ASSEMBLAGES
it reports — NOT tagging text yet, just identifying what the assemblages are.

The variables below follow the ROCEEH Out of Africa Database (ROAD), the
standard research database for this literature, so that what you extract lines
up with an existing framework rather than an ad-hoc one:

  LOCALITY      the specific excavated site an assemblage came from —
                "Koimilot (GnJh-74)", "MAK33", "Panga ya Saidi".
                NOT the basin, pan, formation, valley, region or country that
                contains it. A paper reporting five excavated sites in the
                Makgadikgadi Basin has five localities — MAK2, MAK14K, MAK14O,
                MAK15, MAK33 — and none of them is called "Makgadikgadi
                Basin". If a name denotes an area containing several sites,
                it is not a locality.
  LAYER         a stratigraphic unit within a locality. ROAD keeps two kinds
                apart and so should you:
                  archaeological_layer — defined by its cultural content
                                         ("the ~10 cm artefact layer",
                                          "Layer 3a", "levels IId-IIf")
                  geological_layer     — defined by sediment/deposit
                                         ("Bedded Tuff Member", "channel sands")
  ASSEMBLAGE    the material recovered from a locality, in a layer where the
                paper defines one. This is the unit you are reporting.
                Assemblages are found in localities; assemblages lie in layers.

WHAT AN ASSEMBLAGE IS HERE:
One assemblage per (locality, layer) combination the paper reports data for.

  - A paper excavating two loci at one site reports TWO assemblages, both with
    the same locality, each with its own layer.
  - A paper excavating five separate sites reports FIVE assemblages, each with
    its own locality — even when it discusses them together as one industry.
  - A paper reporting one undivided collection from one site reports ONE
    assemblage, with the locality named and the layer left null. This is
    common and correct; it is not a failure and not a reason to return nothing.
  - Where the paper gives no layer subdivision, leave the layer null rather
    than inventing one. Surface scatters and deflated sites often have none.

THE INDUSTRY IS NOT AN ASSEMBLAGE — IT IS A FIELD ON ONE.
An industry, culture or technocomplex ("Middle Stone Age", "Aurignacian",
"Later Stone Age", "Oldowan") classifies assemblages; it does not contain them
the way a locality contains a layer. Record it in `archaeological_stratigraphy`
on each assemblage it applies to. Do NOT emit a separate assemblage for the
industry itself, even when the paper's title and abstract are framed around it
— a reader wanting the industry-level view gets it by grouping assemblages on
this field, and that view is recoverable, whereas per-locality detail collapsed
into a single industry-level entry is not.

So a paper on "the Makgadikgadi MSA industry" spanning five excavated sites
yields five assemblages, each with archaeological_stratigraphy "Middle Stone
Age" — not six entries, and not one.

RECONCILE BEFORE YOU REPORT.
The evidence reaches you through several channels (headings, prose, whole
tables, figure captions), so the same assemblage
often appears under more than one spelling, and OCR adds further variants.
These are ONE assemblage, not several:
  - numeral style      "Trench 1" / "Trench I";  "Layer 2" / "Layer II"
  - case and spacing   "Locus 1" / "LOCUS 1" / "Locus1"
  - abbreviation       "Middle Stone Age" / "MSA" for the same material
  - OCR damage         an en-dash misread as a letter: "Layers 3-1" -> "3 e 1"
Merge them, report the label the paper uses most consistently, cleaned of
obvious OCR damage, and list what you merged in `also_known_as`. If unsure
whether two names denote different assemblages or one, prefer merging.

Return ONLY a JSON object. `cluster_id` is the identifier the tagging pass will
use, so every assemblage needs one:
{
  "clusters": [
    {
      "cluster_id": "c1",
      "label_as_reported": "<assemblage as the paper names it>",
      "also_known_as": ["<merged variants>"],
      "locality": "<site name, as printed>",
      "archaeological_layer": "<layer defined by cultural content, or null>",
      "geological_layer": "<sediment/deposit unit, or null>",
      "archaeological_stratigraphy": "<industry/culture, e.g. 'Middle Stone Age', or null>",
      "n_artefacts": <integer or null>,
      "evidence": ["<channels that named it, e.g. 'TABLES', 'FIGURE CAPTIONS'>"]
    }
  ]
}
