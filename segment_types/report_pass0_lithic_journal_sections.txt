ABOUT THE CORPUS:
These are standard IMRaD-structured journal articles (Introduction,
Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusions), though exact
headings vary. The underlying sections are consistent:

- FRONT_MATTER: Journal branding, title, author list, affiliations,
  "article info" boxes, table of contents listings. Always at the very
  start.

- ABSTRACT: The brief summary at the article's start. May be labeled
  "Abstract" or "Summary".

- BACKGROUND: General introduction and literature review NOT specific to
  this site's own stratigraphy or excavation — regional context, prior
  research elsewhere, theoretical framing. Typically "1. Introduction" and
  early numbered subsections about the broader research problem.

- SITE_CONTEXT: Description of the excavated site itself — its location,
  stratigraphy, excavation units, dating, chronology, depositional layers.
  THIS IS WHERE THE PAPER USUALLY DEFINES THE LAYERS/ASSEMBLAGES the rest
  of the paper analyzes — treat it as important, not boilerplate, even
  though it's often still under an "Introduction" or "1.2 Site and
  environment"-style heading.

- METHODS: How fieldwork, lab analysis, or lithic classification were
  conducted — typology used, how retouch/platform preparation was scored,
  raw material sourcing methods, refitting, dating methods.

- RESULTS: The substantive lithic findings — artifact frequencies, core
  reduction, flake attributes, retouched tool description, raw material
  analysis, use-wear. Usually the longest section, often with numbered
  subsections. This is the primary target of this pipeline.

- DISCUSSION: Interpretation and comparison — including comparison BETWEEN
  the paper's own assemblages/layers, and comparison with other sites.
  Comparative discussion often contains exactly the cross-context
  statements this pipeline needs to catch, so treat it as important
  alongside Results, not as background.

- CONCLUSIONS: Final summary/conclusions section.

- BOILERPLATE_END: Declaration of competing interest, Acknowledgments,
  References, Supplementary Online Material.

  A PAGE CARRYING TABLE ROWS IS NEVER BOILERPLATE_END. If the inventory
  marks a page `[N table rows]`, that page holds data — artefact counts,
  attribute measurements, raw material breakdowns — and it belongs to
  RESULTS, whatever it is titled and wherever it sits in the document.
  This applies specifically to:
    - "Extended Data Table" / "Supplementary Table" pages in Nature-format
      articles, which carry the paper's actual assemblage data even though
      they are printed after the references;
    - data tables placed after the bibliography, which is normal in
      preprints and author manuscripts.
  Assigning such a page to boilerplate_end discards the paper's primary
  quantitative evidence. A reference list, an acknowledgements paragraph or
  a competing-interests statement has no table rows, so this rule costs
  nothing on genuine back matter.
